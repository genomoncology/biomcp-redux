Where does BioMCP <Fact k="version" /> get its data, and may you reuse it? BioMCP reads <Fact k="counts.sources" /> public sources. It stores none of their data on a server of its own. Each reply names the sources it asked and their terms. The [sources reference](/reference/data-sources/) lists every source with its terms page, key and commands.

## The short answer

Every source is on by default. Each source belongs to one use group. If you use BioMCP commercially, turn off the `noncommercial` and `restricted` groups and check the remaining sources' terms yourself. The publisher's terms are authoritative.

## Use groups

| Group | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `open` | Public-domain or unrestricted data, such as CC0. |
| `attribution` | Reuse requires attribution, such as CC BY. |
| `noncommercial` | Commercial use needs separate permission from the publisher. |
| `restricted` | Source-specific or mixed rights. Check the publisher's terms before reuse. |

A group describes the source's terms as reviewed for BioMCP. It does not grant rights. Public API access grants no extra reuse rights, and many sources carry records with their own licenses, such as an article's license in PMC.

## Turn sources off

`--disable-sources` takes source names and group names, separated by commas. With both groups off, the gene card keeps HGNC and skips MyGene.info and OncoKB:

<Example id="disable-sources" />

For a lasting setting, put the list in `config.json`:

```json
{"disable_sources": ["noncommercial", "restricted"]}
```

The command option and the configuration combine. A disabled source sends no request, and its data cannot come back through the cache. [Keys and configuration](/getting-started/api-keys/) shows where `config.json` lives.

## Sources that need a key

OncoKB and NCI need your own key. NCBI, Semantic Scholar, ORCID and openFDA work without a key at a public rate limit, and a key raises it. BioMCP never buys a license or a key for you, and a source that needs your account sends nothing without it. [Keys and configuration](/getting-started/api-keys/#set-a-key) shows how to set one.

## Sources that read local bundles

HGNC, ClinGen, WikiPathways, cBioPortal studies, EMA, DDInter, WHO finished products, WHO diagnostics, GTR and the FDA companion diagnostics list come as downloadable bundles. [Local data](/local-data/) explains them.
