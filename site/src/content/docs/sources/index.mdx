Where does BioMCP <Fact k="version" /> get its data, and may you reuse it? BioMCP reads <Fact k="counts.sources" /> public sources. It stores none of their data on a server of its own. Each reply names the sources it asked and their terms. The [table below](#every-source) lists every source with its use group, key and terms, and links to its page.

## The short answer

Every source is on by default. Each source belongs to one use group. A use group is a reviewed label for the source's terms; it grants no rights. You are responsible for the licenses your use needs: read each source's terms, which the [table below](#every-source) links. If you use BioMCP commercially, turn off the `noncommercial` and `restricted` groups, then read the terms of the sources that remain. The publisher's terms are authoritative.

## Use groups

The registry defines four groups. Its schema calls each group a "reviewed source-use category, without inferring rights in individual records".

| Group | The registry's definition | What the group holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `open` | "Public-domain or unrestricted source data." | Mostly CC0 and US government public-domain data. Several sources carry conditions; see [open sources with conditions](#open-sources-with-conditions). |
| `attribution` | "Attribution is required." | Sources that require credit. Some add terms, such as ChEMBL's share-alike license. |
| `noncommercial` | "Commercial use requires separate permission." | Sources whose terms reserve commercial use. |
| `restricted` | "Source-specific or mixed rights require checking the publisher's terms." | Restricted: sources with source-specific, record-specific or unreviewed rights. Check the publisher's terms before any reuse. |

### Open sources with conditions

The `open` group's definition does not hold for every source in it. Cellosaurus is CC BY 4.0 and asks for a citation. The FDA and openFDA sources keep third-party rights and the original rights in labels. GTR keeps submitter and third-party rights. SEER has its own website terms. ## Turn sources off

`--disable-sources` takes source names and group names, separated by commas. With both groups off, the gene card keeps HGNC and skips MyGene.info and OncoKB:

<Example id="disable-sources" />

For a lasting setting, put the list in `config.json`:

```json
{"disable_sources": ["noncommercial", "restricted"]}
```

The command option and the configuration combine. A disabled source sends no request, and its data cannot come back through the cache. [Keys and configuration](/getting-started/api-keys/) shows where `config.json` lives.

## Sources that need a key

OncoKB and NCI need your own key. NCBI, Semantic Scholar and openFDA work without a key at a public rate limit, and a key raises it. ORCID takes an optional token. BioMCP never buys a license or a key for you, and a source that needs your account sends nothing without it. [Keys and configuration](/getting-started/api-keys/#set-a-key) shows how to set one.

## Sources that read local bundles

HGNC, ClinGen, WikiPathways, cBioPortal studies, EMA, DDInter, WHO finished products, WHO diagnostics, GTR and the FDA companion diagnostics list come as downloadable bundles. [Local data](/local-data/) explains them.
