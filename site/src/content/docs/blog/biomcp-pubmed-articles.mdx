In 2002, Davies and colleagues reported BRAF mutations in human cancer in Nature. This tutorial follows that paper with BioMCP <Fact k="version" />: back to the papers it cites, forward to the papers that cite it, and into the open full text of one citing trial report. Four steps, three sources: PubMed for the cards, iCite for the citation links and PMC for the full text.

## Before you start

- BioMCP installed; see [install](/getting-started/installation/).
- A network that reaches PubMed, iCite and PMC. No key is needed.
- `jq`, to trim each reply to the fields shown.

## 1. Read the paper's card

`get article` takes a PMID, a PMCID or a DOI:

<Example id="blog-paper-card" />

PubMed gives the title, journal, date and DOI. `pmcid` is null: the paper has no PMC copy, so its full text is not in PMC's open access set.

## 2. Walk back through its references

`article references` lists the PubMed papers the article cites:

<Example id="blog-paper-references" />

iCite links the 24 references, and PubMed supplies each card. The first page holds a 1985 paper on RAS and the 2001 report of a BCR-ABL inhibitor. `--offset 5` returns the next page.

## 3. Walk forward through its citing papers

`article citations` lists the PubMed papers that cite it:

<Example id="blog-paper-citations" />

iCite counts more than 8,000 citing papers. The fourth row is the 2011 phase 3 report of vemurafenib in melanoma with BRAF V600E. The count is iCite's; Semantic Scholar counts differently and is available with `--source semantic-scholar`.

**Checkpoint.** The first line shows a count in the thousands, and every row has a PMID.

## 4. Search a citing paper's full text

The vemurafenib report has a PMCID, so PMC holds its open full text. `article text --find` returns the parts of the text that hold a word:

<Example id="blog-paper-follow" />

PMC's open access copy has eight passages that mention V600E. Each hit names the headings above it; a hit with no heading sits before the first section heading. Add `--save FILE` to keep the text, as [literature](/how-to/find-articles/#4-read-the-full-text) shows.

## What you have

| Step | Source | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card | PubMed | PMID 12068308, DOI and journal |
| References | iCite and PubMed | 24 cited papers |
| Citing papers | iCite and PubMed | the citing count and the first page |
| Full text | PMC | eight V600E passages in PMC3549296 |

A citation count measures attention, not correctness. BioMCP returns the links as iCite records them and does not rank papers by quality.

## Try one change

Run step 3 with `--offset 100` and compare the publication years with the first page.
