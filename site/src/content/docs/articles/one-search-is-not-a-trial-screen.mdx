Ask an agent for recruiting melanoma trials for BRAF V600E and it will run one search, get a short list, and call it done. The list is real. It is also a fraction of what the registry holds about BRAF in melanoma. I ran five searches of ClinicalTrials.gov with BioMCP 1.0 to show the gap.

## Five searches, one question

Every search below keeps `--condition melanoma --status RECRUITING` and changes one filter. `--count-only` returns the registry's total without rows.

The columns are ClinicalTrials.gov's count and the filter. The counts are from the run when this page was built, so they move as the registry changes. In this run, the exact variant text found the fewest trials, the gene found more, and words in the eligibility criteria found more again.

## Why the counts differ

BioMCP sends your words to the registry as written. It does not expand "BRAF V600E" into "V600", "BRAF-mutant" or a drug name. That is deliberate. A tool that rewrites queries hides its reasoning, and when it is wrong you cannot tell.

So the registry's text decides. A sponsor may write "BRAF V600E/K", "BRAF-mutated" or "BRAF V600 mutation", and put it in the title, the conditions, the keywords or only in the eligibility text. Each search reaches a different slice:

- `--variant` searches the variant words in the registry's text fields.
- `--gene` resolves the gene through HGNC and searches its symbol.
- `--criteria` searches the eligibility text, where molecular requirements usually live.
- The condition alone is the ceiling for this question.

The broader searches also catch trials that mention BRAF for another reason, such as an exclusion criterion. More rows is not more answers. It is more candidates to read.

## What a complete screen takes

1. Run the exact search, then the broader ones: the variant's short forms, the gene, the eligibility text.
2. Follow every page of each search until the registry has no more.
3. Read each candidate's eligibility section: `biomcp get trial NCT eligibility`.
4. Record every query, its date, and any source that failed.

A reply's `complete` status describes that one reply. It does not mean the screen is complete. The [clinical trials guide](/how-to/find-trials/#make-a-screen-complete) keeps these steps next to the commands.

## What BioMCP does not decide

None of this decides who is eligible for a trial. The registry text is the sponsor's, and a listing is not an enrollment recommendation. BioMCP gets the records, keeps them traceable, and leaves the judgment to people who can make it.
